THE NATURAL WORLD / 000

The art of survival.

Discover the remarkable adaptations, fascinating behaviors, and extraordinary diversity of reptiles.

01 / INTRODUCTION

Ancient and extraordinary.

Reptiles are vertebrate animals with a long evolutionary history. Modern reptiles include lizards, snakes, turtles, crocodilians, and the tuatara.

Most reptiles are ectothermic, meaning they rely heavily on their surroundings to regulate body temperature. Their scales help protect their bodies and reduce water loss.

Different species have developed remarkable survival strategies, including camouflage, defensive displays, specialized hunting techniques, and even gliding through the air.

Nature has produced an extraordinary variety of solutions to the challenge of survival.

SPECIES PROFILE / 001

Frilled-Neck Lizard.

A remarkable reptile recognized for its dramatic defensive display.

SCIENTIFIC NAME

Chlamydosaurus kingii

HABITAT

Woodlands and tropical savannas

LOCATION

Northern Australia and New Guinea

01 / THE SPECIES

A dramatic defense.

The frilled-neck lizard is a tree-dwelling reptile best known for the large fold of skin surrounding its neck.

When threatened, the lizard can extend its frill, open its mouth, and hiss. This makes the animal appear larger and may discourage predators.

02 / BEHAVIOR

Life among the trees.

Frilled-neck lizards spend much of their time climbing trees and searching for food. Their diet consists primarily of insects and other small animals.

When escaping danger on the ground, they may run on their hind legs for short distances.

INTERESTING FACT: The frill is also used in territorial displays and communication.

SPECIES PROFILE / 002

Flying Snake.

An extraordinary reptile capable of gliding through the forest canopy.

SCIENTIFIC GROUP

Chrysopelea

HABITAT

Tropical forests and wooded areas

LOCATION

South and Southeast Asia

01 / THE SPECIES

Flight without wings.

Flying snakes are a group of tree-dwelling snakes known for their ability to glide between elevated locations.

Although they cannot truly fly, they can travel impressive distances through the air by flattening their bodies and controlling their movements.

02 / ADAPTATIONS

Built to glide.

When gliding, these snakes spread their ribs outward to create a flattened, curved body shape that generates aerodynamic forces.

They also move from side to side while airborne, helping maintain stability. Their diet includes lizards, frogs, and other small vertebrates.

INTERESTING FACT: Some flying snakes can glide tens of meters between trees.

SPECIES PROFILE / 003

The Chameleon.

A master of visual communication, precise hunting, and adaptation.

SCIENTIFIC FAMILY

Chamaeleonidae

HABITAT

Forests, shrublands, and grasslands

LOCATION

Primarily Africa and Madagascar

01 / THE SPECIES

Masters of adaptation.

Chameleons are specialized lizards known for their unusual eyes, long tongues, and ability to change color.

Many species live in trees and shrubs. Their specialized feet help them grip branches, while some species use their tails for additional support.

02 / UNIQUE ABILITIES

Color and vision.

Chameleons change color for several reasons, including communication and temperature regulation. They do not simply copy the colors of every background.

Their eyes can move independently, allowing them to observe different directions. They also use rapidly extending tongues to capture prey.

INTERESTING FACT: Chameleons can coordinate both eyes to accurately judge the distance to their prey.
